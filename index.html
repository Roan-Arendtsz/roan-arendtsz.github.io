About

I am a fourth-year undergraduate student at Carleton College. I am interested in elementary descriptions of the universe, in particular string theory and other theories pertaining to the quantum gravity project.

My research interests span several subtopics of quantum gravity. I am interested in extending the vast array of tools developed and lessons learned from the AdS/CFT duality to new contexts. One such context which I am interested in exploring is asymptotically flat spacetimes, which model our universe much better than AdS spacetimes. Moreover, I am also interested in the extension of the holographic principle to characterize non-conformal field theories such as QCD. The gravitational path integral, and the numerous issues which remain in its development, is another area of research which I am drawn towards. Finally, I am interested in the cosmological constant problem, and more generally the integration of dark energy within quantum gravity.

Research

I am currently working with Professor Jay Tasson in extending a method his research group developed for improving constraints of Lorentz violation in the proton, neutron, and electron sector of the standard model to the neutrino sector. This Beyond the Standard Model project has involved a literature review of Lorentz violations of neutrinos, both for the underlying theory and the physical experiments which have been previously used to constrain the violation. I am now working on reinterpreting the method and code developed in previous years to the context of neutrinos.

I have previously worked on identifying and correcting systematic trends in stellar spectroscopy data. I developed a method which improved the short-term precision of spectroscopic measurements up to 3.2x. This has helped disentangle signals in the spectra originating from stellar magnetic activity versus doppler shifts from palentary motion, and is thus valuable for confirming the existence of planets. The method has been used in several other published studies (see CV). A manuscript has been developed related to the method itself, and after receiving a referee report, a revised version of the manuscript has been submitted to The Astronomical Journal. I have presented these findings in many teleconference meetings and at several poster sessions.

Exercise Compilations

I have found that the most direct way to learn physics is to work on problems. There were several courses throughout my undergraduate education which I was unable to take for various reasons, and which in hindsight I wish I had taken. Spurred by a desire to fulfill this gap in my education, I have compiled a series of exercises which I have worked out by myself. These projects all remain in progress, and will be updated as I have time to work on the exercises. The topics which I have chosen are Classical Mechanics, Complex Analysis, and Lie Groups.
